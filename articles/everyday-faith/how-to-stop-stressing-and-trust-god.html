How to Stop Stressing and Trust God with Your Problems

Faith & Peace ROLCC Pastoral Team 25 June 2026 6 min read 2 Chronicles 20:15

Summary

We often carry all the stress before a crisis, then take all the credit after God brings us through. God offers a better way: the battle belongs to Him, and we show up in faith while leaving the outcome in His hands.

We've all been there: an important interview is coming up, a major bill is due, or a relationship is on the rocks. Your heart starts racing, you play out every worst-case scenario in your mind, and you stay up all night trying to figure out a backup plan. You are entirely in "fight-or-flight" mode.

But notice what happens once the problem is solved. You pass the test, you get the job, or the situation settles down. When a friend asks how it went, it's easy to say, "Oh, I crushed it! I just used a few clever strategies."

We completely forget how terrified we were just days before. This is the trap of self-reliance: we carry 100% of the stress when we're in the foxhole, but we take 100% of the credit once we win.

If you are tired of living in this stressful cycle, God offers a much better way to live.

What the Bible Says About Your Stress

The Bible addresses this exact struggle in a famous verse from 2 Chronicles 20:15:

Do not be afraid or dismayed because of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours, but God's.

2 Chronicles 20:15

When we try to fight our own battles, we usually fail or burn out because we weren't designed to carry that weight. Look at the history of God's people in the Old Testament. When they faced the massive, intimidating walls of Jericho, God didn't tell them to build giant ladders or weapons. He told them to march around the city and trust Him. The walls fell down without a conventional fight.

Yet, a few generations later, people started rewriting history. Veterans would sit around telling stories about their own reflexes and sword skills rather than pointing to God's miracle. When we forget who gave us the victory, our pride grows, and with pride comes even more anxiety the next time a crisis hits.

The Lesson of Gideon's 300

A perfect example of God breaking this stressful, self-reliant habit is the story of Gideon in the book of Judges. Israel was being bullied and starved by a massive enemy army called the Midianites. Gideon managed to gather 30,000 men to fight back.

But God looked at the army and said there were too many people. Why? Because if they won, they would brag and claim, "We saved ourselves by our own power."

  • The Fear Test: Gideon told everyone who was secretly terrified to go home. Instantly, 20,000 men walked away. Only 10,000 were left.
  • The Pride Test: God narrowed it down even further, leaving Gideon with a tiny group of just 300 men against a giant army.

God deliberately stripped away their human strength so they would have no choice but to rely on Him. When the battle was won, nobody could take the credit except God.

3 Practical Steps to Let God Take the Reins

Surrendering your stress to God doesn't mean you become lazy or do nothing. Gideon still had to show up to the battlefield. Giving the battle to God simply means you stop carrying the emotional weight of the outcome.

1. Write Down Your "Emotion Equation"

The next time you feel panicky about an interview, a medical report, or a family issue, open a notebook or a notes app on your phone. Write down exactly how you feel: your fears, your lack of control, and your raw prayer to God.

When God carries you through it, look back at what you wrote. This keeps you honest. It stops you from saying "I did it" and reminds you that God was the one who actually made a way.

2. Practice Listening to the Shepherd

Gideon didn't navigate his crisis by guessing; he learned to recognize God's voice in his life. We often stress because we are listening to too many worldly voices, news headlines, or social media opinions.

Make it a habit to open your Bible daily and get plugged into a local church or small group fellowship. When you consistently study the Word with others, you learn to naturally recognize the peaceful voice of Jesus over the noisy chatter of anxiety.

3. Build a "Testimony Bank"

Every time God provides for you, treat it as a permanent piece of evidence for your faith. A true believer uses past victories as fuel for future trust. When a brand-new problem hits you next month, you don't have to panic. You can look at your track record with God and say:

He handled my past, He is in control of my today, and He already has tomorrow figured out.

Frequently Asked Questions

If the battle belongs to God, why am I still facing so many problems?

God never promised a life without battles; He promised that you wouldn't have to fight them alone. Difficulties are often the exact ground God uses to build your faith and show His power to the people around you.

Is it wrong that I feel afraid?

Fear is a normal human emotion. Gideon was terrified, and many of the great heroes of the Bible struggled with fear. The goal isn't to never feel fear, but to stop letting fear dictate your choices.

How do I know if I'm trusting God or just being irresponsible?

Irresponsibility means avoiding your duties and hoping they magically disappear. Trusting God means doing what is practically in your power to do (studying for the test, applying for jobs, communicating kindly) while completely leaving the final results in God's hands.

If this article spoke to you, you are welcome at River of Life Christian Church. Come as you are, and take your next step at your own pace — whether that is visiting on Sunday, joining Cell Fellowship, or simply asking someone to pray with you.

Key takeaways

  • Catch yourself bragging — give thanks to God when things go right.
  • Start a testimony journal to track anxieties before and faithfulness after.
  • Stop waiting for perfect conditions; you need God, not a massive army of resources.
  • Get into a small group so the Word takes root beyond Sunday.
