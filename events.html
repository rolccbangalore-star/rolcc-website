Related FAQs

Quick answers related to this part of our church life.

Yes. It’s completely okay to come alone. Many people visit ROLCC by themselves—especially when they’re new to the city or exploring faith. We’ll welcome you warmly, but we won’t put you on the spot or ask you to introduce yourself publicly. You can sit, listen, and leave quietly if you prefer. If you’d like help finding a smaller circle, we can point you to a Cell Fellowship where friendships often form more naturally during the week.

For a Christian, regular fellowship isn’t optional—it’s an important part of growth. If you’re new to faith, it’s okay to begin at a pace you can sustain, but we don’t want “occasional church” to become the goal. We understand some seasons make Sundays difficult (work shifts, travel, health, family). In those times, stay actively connected through Cell Fellowship and other spiritual enrichment during the week—prayer gatherings, serving, training, and community—so you’re still being strengthened and supported, not drifting alone.

That’s okay. You don’t have to be “ready” to get involved on day one. At ROLCC, you can simply attend, listen, and take your time. We’ll welcome you, but we won’t pressure you to join a group, serve, or share publicly. When you’re ready for a next step, you can choose something small—fill the Welcome Form, ask for prayer, or try a Cell Fellowship for a few weeks. Healthy involvement grows best through trust, not force.

Yes. If you just want to listen, that’s completely okay. At ROLCC, you’re welcome to sit, observe, and process things at your own pace—especially if you’re exploring faith or returning after a break. You won’t be asked to introduce yourself publicly or do anything that makes you uncomfortable. If you ever want support, you can ask for prayer through our Prayer Ministry, speak with a pastor, or connect via the Welcome Form—only if you choose.

Start with one consistent place where you can see the same people regularly. In Bangalore, community is built through rhythms, not random meetups. A simple step is to attend church weekly and join a Cell Fellowship—smaller groups are where friendships grow faster. At ROLCC, you can connect through the Welcome Form, meet someone from our team after service, and find a group or serving team that fits you (Welcoming, Media, Outreach, etc.). Show up consistently for a few weeks—belonging usually follows.

Start with one safe connection point and take one step at a time. If you’re new and don’t know anyone, begin by attending a Sunday service regularly, then use the Welcome Form so someone can help you get connected. A Cell Fellowship is often the best next step because it’s smaller, relational, and easier to talk. If you’re open to it, serving in a team (Welcoming, Media/Production, Outreach, etc.) also helps you meet people naturally. You don’t have to do it all at once—just start somewhere.

Homesickness is real, and it doesn’t mean you’re weak—it means you loved where you came from. A practical start is to build a small, steady routine: sleep well, eat well, stay connected to family back home, and don’t isolate yourself. Community helps your heart heal, not just time. At ROLCC, joining a Cell Fellowship can give you people who notice you and pray with you. If the sadness feels heavy or long-lasting, our Counselling Ministry can also support you with care and wisdom.
